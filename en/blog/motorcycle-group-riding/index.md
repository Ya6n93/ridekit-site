Riding in a group starts with riding as if you were alone: your gap, your line and your overtakes remain your own responsibility. The group adds organisation (who leads, who sweeps, where everyone sits) and a shared language. France's road safety agency, the [Sécurité routière](https://www.securite-routiere.gouv.fr/chacun-son-mode-de-deplacement/dangers-de-la-route-moto/mieux-conduire-moto/conduire-en-groupe-moto), puts it simply: before setting off, agree on how you will communicate about position, gaps, maximum speed, stops and emergencies.

## Staggered formation

On a straight road wide enough, ride staggered: the leader in the left part of the lane, the second rider in the right part, the third on the left, and so on ([Mutuelle des Motards](https://www.mutuelledesmotards.fr/actualites/balades-de-printemps-moto-comment-bien-rouler-en-groupe)). Everyone sees further ahead, stays visible in the mirrors of the rider in front, and keeps an escape space to the side if that rider brakes hard ([Matmut](https://www.matmut.fr/assurance/moto/conseils/rouler-groupe-moto)).

Staggering is not a way to close up: your safe gap is to the bike directly ahead of you, offset or not.

**Go single file** in bends (everyone takes their own line, then the stagger reforms), on narrow roads, and whenever grip or visibility drops: gravel, rain, crests, roadworks. Never ride side by side: if your neighbour swerves or falls, you have nowhere to go.

## How big a gap?

The French Highway Code ([article R412-12](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000051682619)) sets the rule for everyone: the safe distance is the distance covered in **at least two seconds**. That is about 28 m at 50 km/h, 44 m at 80 km/h and 50 m at 90 km/h. Check it by counting "one and two" as the bike ahead passes a post. Leave much more in the rain.

On motorways and dual carriageways, don't ride as a pack: the Mutuelle des Motards advises 150 to 200 m between bikes so other traffic can merge.

**The accordion effect**: when the leader brakes, the slowdown grows bike after bike until the last one has to slam on the brakes. The cure is a smooth leader who slows early and accelerates gently ([ViaMichelin](https://www.viamichelin.fr/magazine/article/moto-nos-conseils-pour-rouler-en-groupe)).

## Lead rider, sweep and order

- **The lead rider** knows the route, sets the pace and picks stops wide enough for everyone. Nobody passes the leader.
- **Right behind**, the least experienced rider or the least powerful bike: the leader paces the group on what they see in the mirror.
- **The sweep** rides last, knows the route and the stops, and never passes anyone. Nobody should ever be behind the sweep.
- **Everyone keeps their place** for the whole ride, and takes it back after each stop.

Keep it small: Matmut advises no more than 8 riders; the Mutuelle des Motards suggests splitting into sub-groups beyond 5, about 5 minutes apart.

## Overtaking: everyone decides alone

Don't overtake within the group. To pass another vehicle, each rider makes their own decision, only when they can complete it safely. Never copy the overtake of the bike ahead: what they saw two seconds ago is no longer true for you. And never overtake just to "catch up" with the group.

## Roundabouts, junctions and toll booths

At a roundabout or a stop sign, the group doesn't go through as a block: everyone gives way as if alone. If the light turns red mid-group, those through wait a little further on, somewhere safe. At French motorway tolls, [VINCI Autoroutes](https://www.vinci-autoroutes.com/fr/conseils/securite/moto-passage-peage/) advises using a green-arrow or card lane, avoiding height-gauge lanes, not bunching on the platform and keeping payment (or a toll tag) easy to reach. Regroup on a rest area, never on the hard shoulder.

## Hand signals

There is no official code: each group agrees on its own at the briefing. Common ones: fist with thumb down pointed at the tank (fuel), index pointing at the road or foot lowered (hazard), raised hand drawing a circle (U-turn), right foot stretched out (thanks). Add at least "I need to stop" and an emergency signal. Only take one hand off the bars on a straight, never while braking or cornering.
