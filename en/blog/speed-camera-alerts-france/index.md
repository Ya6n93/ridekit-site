If you ride to France, leave your speed camera warnings at home, or switch them off. French law bans owning, carrying or using any device that detects speed cameras **or tells you where they are**. What a sat nav or an app may show instead is a **danger zone**: a stretch of road of at least 300 m in built-up areas, 2 km outside them and 4 km on motorways, which may or may not contain a camera. Whatever you use, the speed limit remains the only rule that counts.

## What the law says

Article R413-15 of the French Highway Code (*Code de la route*) punishes owning, carrying or using a device designed to detect speed enforcement equipment or disrupt it: radar **detectors** and **jammers**. A decree of 3 January 2012 extended the ban to devices that **warn of or show the location** of that equipment: speed camera **warning** devices and app features. France's highest administrative court, the Conseil d'État, upheld it in 2013 ([NAVX decision](https://www.conseil-etat.fr/fr/arianeweb/CE/decision/2013-03-06/355815)).

| What you have or do | Status | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Radar detector | Banned (R413-15) | Fine up to €1,500, 6 points off a French licence, licence suspension up to 3 years, device seized; the vehicle can be seized if the device is fitted to it |
| Device or app showing camera locations | Banned since 2012 | Same penalties |
| Jammer used on the road | Banned | Same penalties |
| Navigation aid showing danger zones only | Allowed | Nothing |

Sources: [ANTAI penalty scale](https://antai.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2021-10/Les_avis_de_contravention-Bareme_points_v2.pdf) and the official commentary of the [Constitutional Council's 2021 decision](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/decision/2021/2021948QPC.htm). The fine applies whether or not the device is switched on when you are stopped.

## Where 300 m, 2 km and 4 km come from

Those lengths are not in the Highway Code. They come from an agreement signed on 28 July 2011 between the French Interior Ministry and the association of driving-aid providers: its members stopped showing the exact location of fixed or mobile cameras and police checks, and show instead "dangerous road sections" of **at least 4 km on motorways, 2 km outside built-up areas and 300 m in built-up areas**, which may or may not contain a camera. As [TomTom's guide](https://download.tomtom.com/open/manuals/dangerzones/refman/TomTom-EU-LIVE-RG-fr-fr.pdf) puts it, the camera "can be anywhere in the zone", and nearby cameras may be merged into one longer zone.

A danger zone isn't always a camera, either: it can also be a level crossing, a road with a bad accident record or a school.

## Reports of police checks

Since 1 November 2021, during certain roadside checks (alcohol and drugs for up to 2 hours; some identity checks and searches for wanted persons for up to 12 hours), the local prefect can order navigation apps to stop relaying user reports within 10 km outside built-up areas or 2 km inside them. **Speed checks are not covered** by this blocking. The Constitutional Council upheld the scheme in November 2021.

> **Good to know.** Rules differ across borders. In Switzerland, for instance, speed camera warning devices, including sat navs with that feature, are banned outright, and customs can seize them ([FOCBS](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/fr/detecteurs-radar-interdits-suisse)). Check before each country.

## How RideKit handles it

RideKit (iPhone, French interface) shows danger zones in France during guidance, for free: a red banner with the zone's speed limit. They are computed on the phone from the official list of fixed speed cameras published by the Interior Ministry on [data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/liste-des-radars-fixes-en-france), as 300 m, 2 km or 4 km stretches. RideKit never shows where a camera is, nor its type, and does not report police checks. Mobile cameras and camera cars are not covered. More in French: [GPS moto](/gps-moto/).

> **Watch out.** A danger zone is not permission to speed elsewhere. Mobile cameras can be anywhere, and the limit applies everywhere. The sign is your guide.
